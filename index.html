01Why this matters

What gets left behind does not stay behind.

Food waste left on tables, floors and common areas affects everyone who uses the hostel after you.

01

Hygiene problems

Exposed food waste contaminates the surfaces students eat on every day.

02

Unpleasant smell

Leftovers turn stale quickly, especially in enclosed lifts and corridors.

03

Insects and pests

Bones, shells and scraps attract flies, cockroaches and rodents.

04

Poor dining conditions

The next student has to clear your table before they can sit down.

05

Extra work for housekeeping

Avoidable mess adds hours to an already full cleaning schedule.

06

An unpleasant place to live

Shared spaces set the standard of the entire hostel.

We are not asking students to clean the hostel. We are asking students not to make it dirty.
02Dining etiquette

Before you leave the table.

Six seconds of checking is all this takes.

  1. Check the table before leaving.
  2. Collect chicken bones and other food waste.
  3. Dispose of eggshells, corn cobs and food leftovers properly.
  4. Put tissues, wrappers and disposable items in the appropriate bins.
  5. Leave the table reasonably clean for the next student.
  6. Do not leave food waste on chairs, tables or surrounding areas.

04Common areas

Shared space is everyone’s space.

Dining hallCorridorsLiftsStaircasesCommon areas

Food waste must not be thrown or left in common areas.

What counts as a violation

  • Leaving food or snacks on lift floors
  • Throwing corn cobs outside
  • Leaving chicken bones on tables
  • Leaving eggshells on dining tables
  • Throwing wrappers or tissues on the floor
  • Deliberately littering common areas

Why these areas in particular

Lifts, staircases and corridors are cleaned on a fixed schedule, not continuously. Anything left there stays there — in front of every student, visitor and parent who passes through.

Common spaces are shared spaces. Keep them clean for everyone.
05Monitoring & accountability

Reviewed when necessary. Handled privately.

Hostel common areas may be monitored through CCTV where surveillance systems are installed. CCTV footage may be reviewed by authorised Hostel Administration and Wardens when investigating cleanliness or hostel-rule violations.

Accessed for a reason

Footage is reviewed by authorised staff, for a specific reported or observed violation — not as routine surveillance of individuals.

Never made public

Identified violations are handled privately and in accordance with hostel rules and university procedures. Footage is not displayed, circulated or shared publicly.

06Progressive enforcement

The purpose is correction, not punishment.

Almost every case ends at the first two stages.

Education → Reminder → Warning → Fine → Disciplinary action

  1. Step 01

    Educate

    Posters, announcements, orientation sessions and dining-area reminders.

  2. Step 02

    Remind

    A private verbal or written reminder for a first observed violation.

  3. Step 03

    Warning

    Repeated or confirmed violations may receive an official warning.

  4. Step 04

    Penalty

    Repeated violations may result in a monetary fine as per hostel regulations. Applicable fines will be imposed and recorded through the University ERP system.

  5. Step 05

    Disciplinary action

    Persistent or serious violations may be referred for further disciplinary action according to university and hostel regulations.

09Housekeeping

Someone has to clean what you leave behind.

Housekeeping staff work continuously to maintain the hostel environment. Leaving food waste on tables, floors or common areas unnecessarily increases their workload.

Cleanliness is not the responsibility of housekeeping alone. It is a shared responsibility.
10Student responsibility

Hostel is your home away from home.

Many students are living independently for the first time. Hostel freedom also comes with responsibility for a space shared with hundreds of others.

Dining etiquette and cleanliness are basic habits that apply everywhere — at home, in restaurants, cafeterias and shared university spaces.

If you wouldn’t leave it on the table at home or in a restaurant, don’t leave it here.
11Who monitors this

Accountability, drawn plainly.

Hostel Administration → Hostel Wardens → Students

Monitoring and enforcement are carried out by Hostel Wardens under the Hostel Administration. Students are not monitors and are not expected to police or report on one another.

Hostel Wardens will

  • Monitor hostel cleanliness and dining etiquette
  • Address reported violations
  • Give reminders and warnings where appropriate
  • Coordinate with housekeeping

And where required

  • Review relevant CCTV footage when authorised
  • Record repeated violations
  • Initiate penalties through the ERP system where applicable
  • Escalate persistent or serious violations per university procedures
